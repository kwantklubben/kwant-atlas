### 1. Intuition & Practical Objective

These three results are the *multipliers* that turn stochastic calculus into a pricing theory. **Feynman–Kac** declares that the PDE picture and the expectation picture are the *same number* — solve a parabolic PDE <==> take a discounted expectation along an SDE. **Martingale Representation (MRT)** says that in a Brownian world *every* martingale is an Itô integral — the mathematical license for *replicating any* derivative with a delta position. The **Fundamental Theorems of Asset Pricing** then assemble it: no arbitrage ⇔ a martingale measure exists, and completeness ⇔ that measure is unique.

Practical objective: understand the complete toolkit that (a) prices a derivative either by PDE or by $\mathbb Q$-expectation, as convenient; (b) makes "every $T$-claim is hedgeable in a complete one-BM market" rigorous; (c) tells you *exactly when* that falls apart (extra randomness, jumps, missing assets) — the theoretical reason real markets need stochastic-vol and jump models.

### 2. Mathematical Ground Truth & Derivations

#### 2.1 Feynman–Kac (Björk Props 5.5/5.6; Shreve II §4.5)
Let $X$ solve $dX_t=\mu(t,X_t)dt+\sigma(t,X_t)dW_t$. If $F(t,x)$ solves the parabolic PDE
$$
F_t+\mu F_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2F_{xx}-rF=0,\qquad F(T,x)=\Phi(x),
$$
then (with discounting, Björk Prop 5.6)
$$
F(t,x)=e^{-r(T-t)}\,\mathbb E_{t,x}\big[\Phi(X_T)\big].
$$
*Applied to the risk-neutral dynamics* ($\mu=r$, $r$ the drift) this says exactly: **the BSM PDE solution equals the discounted $\mathbb Q$-expectation of the payoff.** The two §05-pricing routes — PDE and expectation — coincide by construction. Nondiscounted form ($r=0$) is Björk Prop 5.5; multidimensional form Prop 5.8.

#### 2.2 Martingale Representation Theorem (Shreve II Thm 5.3.1; Björk Thm 11.2; Shreve I §18)
Let $\mathcal F(t)$ be the filtration generated by a BM $W$. Then **every** $\mathcal F$-martingale $M$ has the representation
$$
M(t)=M(0)+\int_0^t\Gamma(u)\,dW(u)
$$
for a unique adapted process $\Gamma$ (in $L^2$ if $M$ is square-integrable). Multidimensional version (Shreve II Thm 5.4.2): $M(t)=M(0)+\int_0^t\Gamma(u)\cdot dW(u)$. **Key accumulation for hedging:** combining MRT with Girsanov (Shreve II Cor 5.3.2), for any $\mathcal F(T)$-claim $V$, the $\mathbb Q$-martingale $M(t)=\tilde{\mathbb E}\big[\tilde D(T)V(T)\mid\mathcal F(t)\big]$ has a representation whose integrand $\Gamma$ is matched by choosing the delta $\Delta(t)=\Gamma(t)/(\sigma(t)D(t)S(t))$ — so the claim is *replicated* (Shreve II §5.3.2). This requires $\sigma\ne0$ (the BM randomness must be in the stock) and $\mathcal F$ generated by $W$ (no extra randomness).

#### 2.3 The Fundamental Theorems (Shreve II Thm 5.4.7/5.4.9; Harrison–Pliska; Björk 10.9/10.17)
Model: $m$ stocks driven by $d$ BMs, $dS_i=S_i\big(\alpha_i\,dt+\sum_j\sigma_{ij}dW_j\big)$. The **market price of risk equations**
$$
\alpha_i(t)-R(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{ij}(t)\,\Theta_j(t),\qquad i=1,\dots,m
$$
(m equations, $d$ unknowns — one per BM, not per stock) drive the classification (Shreve II §5.4):

| Market-price-of-risk system | Risk-neutral measure | Market |
|---|---|---|
| unique solution | unique (complete) | no arbitrage + **complete** (every claim hedgeable) |
| no solution | none | **arbitrage** exists |
| multiple solutions | multiple | no arbitrage but **incomplete** |

- **First FT (Shreve II 5.4.7):** no arbitrage ⇔ a risk-neutral measure exists (the MPR system is solvable).
- **Second FT (Shreve II 5.4.9):** market complete ⇔ the risk-neutral measure is unique (⇔ the $m\times d$ volatility matrix has full rank / invertible square case $m=d$).

The **meta-theorem** (Björk 8.3.1) encapsulates the counting: with $M$ risky assets and $R$ random sources, arbitrage-free ⇔ $M\le R$ (fewer/equal assets than risks), complete ⇔ $M\ge R$ (enough assets to hedge), both ⇔ $M=R$. BSM has $M=R=1$ ⇒ complete and arbitrage-free; a jump process adds a distinct random source so one stock can no longer hedge it.

### 3. Computational Implementation — verify the bridges

Three stdlib checks: (a) Feynman–Kac — the $\mathbb Q$-expectation of a payoff equals the BSM PDE solution (MC vs closed form); (b) martingale representation — delta-replicating a written call, the discrete hedge P&L closes to $\approx0$ at expiry (the replication story made concrete); (c) the completeness counting rule.

```python
import math, random
random.seed(201)

def bsm_call(S,X,T,r,sig):
    d1=(math.log(S/X)+(r+0.5*sig*sig)*T)/(sig*math.sqrt(T)); d2=d1-sig*math.sqrt(T)
    return S*0.5*(1+math.erf(d1/math.sqrt(2))) - X*math.exp(-r*T)*0.5*(1+math.erf(d2/math.sqrt(2)))

S0, X, T, r, sig = 100.0, 100.0, 1.0, 0.05, 0.20

# (a) Feynman-Kac: F(0,S0)=e^-rT E^Q[Phi(S_T)] == PDE solution (BSM closed form)
tot=0.0
for _ in range(300000):
    Wn=random.gauss(0, math.sqrt(T))
    ST=S0*math.exp((r-0.5*sig*sig)*T + sig*Wn)
    tot += max(ST-X, 0.0)
fk_mc = tot/300000*math.exp(-r*T)
print("Feynman-Kac  MC  F(0,S0)=e^-rT E^Q[Phi] = %.4f" % fk_mc)
print("Feynman-Kac  PDE (BSM closed-form)      = %.4f" % bsm_call(S0,X,T,r,sig))

# (b) Martingale representation / hedging: replicate a written call, P&L -> 0
def delta_hedge(n):
    dt = T/n
    S = [S0]
    for _ in range(n):
        S.append(S[-1]*math.exp((r-0.5*sig*sig)*dt + sig*random.gauss(0, math.sqrt(dt))))
    bank = bsm_call(S0, X, T, r, sig)      # receive premium for written call
    hold = 0.0
    for i in range(n):                     # rebalance to delta = N(d1) each step
        tau = T - i*dt; St = S[i]
        d1 = (math.log(St/X)+(r+0.5*sig*sig)*tau)/(sig*math.sqrt(tau))
        delta = 0.5*(1+math.erf(d1/math.sqrt(2)))
        bank = (bank - (delta-hold)*St) * math.exp(r*dt); hold = delta
    pnl = bank + hold*S[-1] - max(S[-1]-X, 0.0)     # unwind + pay claim
    return S[-1], pnl
random.seed(77)
ST, pnl = delta_hedge(500)
print("Martingale rep. (delta-replication): S_T=%.2f  hedge P&L = %.4f (theory 0)" % (ST, pnl))
```
```
Feynman-Kac  MC  F(0,S0)=e^-rT E^Q[Phi] = 10.4550
Feynman-Kac  PDE (BSM closed-form)      = 10.4506
Martingale rep. (delta-replication): S_T=80.84  hedge P&L = 0.0151 (theory 0)
```

### 4. Failure Modes & First-Principles Breakdowns

1. **MRT needs the BM filtration.** Both MRT and completeness/hedging fall apart if the filtration is larger than that generated by $W$ (e.g. an independent Poisson, an untraded factor). Replicating an $\mathcal F(T)$-claim then requires a hedge $h$ with an extra, unhedgeable source of randomness — the deep root of market incompleteness and the reason models with jumps/SV need completion choices (Shreve II §5.3; Björk §14.2).
2. **$\sigma=0$ destroys completeness.** If the stock has no diffusion, the BM randomness is not in the tradable; you cannot hedge with something that has no volatility. The MRT-integrand-matching $\Delta=\Gamma/(\sigma DS)$ divides by $\sigma$ (Shreve II §5.3.2).
3. **Feynman–Kac under the physical measure is mispricing.** The expectation must be along the *risk-neutral* drift ($r$), not $\mu$. Running the SDE with drift $\mu$ and plugging into $e^{-rT}\mathbb E[\Phi]$ breaks the PDE↔expectation correspondence ([[foundations/stochastic-calculus/05-girsanov-and-risk-neutral|05 · Girsanov]]).
4. **Confusing "prices fixed" with "model correct."** Completeness fixes the *price* of every claim, but only within that model's distributional assumptions. It guarantees hedgeability, never realism — it is the precondition for the smiles/jumps/SV extensions, not a vindication of BSM (see [[pillars/03-derivative-pricing/black-scholes-merton/05-failure-modes-and-practice|BSM 05 · Failure Modes]]).
