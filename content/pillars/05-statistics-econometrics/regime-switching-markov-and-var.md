# VAR & Markov Regime-Switching Models

Financial markets do not operate under static parameters. They shift between distinct economic regimes: calm bull markets, inflation shocks, and liquidity sell-offs.

## 1. Vector Autoregression (VAR(p))
Models dynamic feedback interactions across multiple time series simultaneously:
$$\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{c} + \sum_{i=1}^p \mathbf{\Phi}_i \mathbf{y}_{t-i} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{\Sigma})$$
- **Impulse Response Function (IRF):** Traces how an unexpected shock to one variable (e.g. Fed Funds rate) cascades through other assets (equities, bond yields, FX) across future quarters.

---

## 2. Hamilton's Markov-Switching Autoregressive Model
Assumes market returns are generated by an unobservable discrete latent state $S_t \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$:
$$r_t = \mu_{S_t} + \sigma_{S_t} \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \sim \text{i.i.d. } \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$
Transitions between states follow a first-order Markov chain:
$$P(S_t = j \mid S_{t-1} = i) = p_{ij}$$
$$\mathbf{P} = \begin{bmatrix} p_{11} & p_{12} \\ p_{21} & p_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} P(\text{Bull} \to \text{Bull}) & P(\text{Bull} \to \text{Bear}) \\ P(\text{Bear} \to \text{Bull}) & P(\text{Bear} \to \text{Bear}) \end{bmatrix}$$

### Quant Strategy Application
Using the Hamilton filter, compute the filtered probability $P(S_t = \text{Bear} \mid \mathcal{F}_t)$ in real-time. When probability exceeds 0.5, dynamically deleverage the portfolio or scale into trend-following CTAs.
