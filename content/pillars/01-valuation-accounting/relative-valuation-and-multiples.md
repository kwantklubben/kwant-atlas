# Relative Valuation & Multiples

Relative valuation prices an asset by comparing its market pricing multiples to those of comparable peer companies.

## 1. Multiples Classification Framework

Multiples must strictly match the denominator to the appropriate claimant:

| Multiple | Numerator | Denominator | Consistent? | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P/E** | Market Cap (Equity) | Net Income (Equity) | ✅ Yes | Traditional earnings valuation |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Enterprise Value (Firm) | EBITDA (Firm) | ✅ Yes | Capital-structure neutral |
| **P/EBITDA** | Market Cap (Equity) | EBITDA (Firm) | ❌ Inconsistent | Flawed (mixes firm cash with equity price) |
| **EV/Sales** | Enterprise Value (Firm) | Revenue (Firm) | ✅ Yes | Top-line cross-industry comparison |
| **P/B** | Market Cap (Equity) | Book Value of Equity | ✅ Yes | Financial institutions & asset-heavy |

---

## 2. Enterprise Value Architecture
$$\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \text{Market Cap} + \text{Total Debt} + \text{Preferred Equity} + \text{Minority Interest} - \text{Cash & Equivalents}$$

### Why Subtract Cash?
Enterprise Value measures the cost to acquire the entire operating business. If an acquirer buys all debt and equity, they immediately acquire the cash balance, reducing net cash outlay required to fund operations.

## 3. Multiple Expansion vs Fundamental Earnings Growth
Total Return for an equity over horizon $T$ decomposes cleanly into two drivers:
$$1 + R = \left( \frac{\text{Multiple}_T}{\text{Multiple}_0} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Earnings}_T}{\text{Earnings}_0} \right)$$
- **Value Trap:** A stock trading at $5\times \text{P/E}$ is not cheap if Earnings are contracting at $-20\%$ per year.
- **Multiple Compression:** A high-flying tech stock trading at $100\times \text{P/E}$ will lose value even with $30\%$ earnings growth if the multiple compresses to $50\times$.
