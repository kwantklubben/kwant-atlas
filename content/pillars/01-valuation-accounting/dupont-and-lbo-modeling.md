# DuPont Decomposition & LBO Modeling

## 1. DuPont Analysis: Deconstructing Return on Equity (ROE)

Return on Equity ($\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}$) is the primary metric of corporate capital efficiency. However, a high ROE can be generated by brilliant operations OR reckless financial leverage. DuPont analysis isolates the exact transmission mechanism.

### The 3-Stage DuPont Equation
$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Revenue}} \right)}_{\text{Net Profit Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}} \right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}} \right)}_{\text{Financial Leverage}}$$

### The 5-Stage Extended DuPont Equation
$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{EBT}} \right)}_{\text{Tax Burden}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}} \right)}_{\text{Interest Burden}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}} \right)}_{\text{Operating Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Assets}} \right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left( \frac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}} \right)}_{\text{Leverage}}$$

- **Quality Signal:** A firm increasing ROE via Operating Margin expansion is an elite business with pricing power.
- **Distress Signal:** A firm maintaining ROE solely by increasing Financial Leverage while Operating Margin deteriorates is entering insolvency risk.

## 2. Leveraged Buyout (LBO) Mechanics

An LBO acquires a target company using a high proportion of debt ($60\%\text{--}80\%$) and a small equity sponsor investment ($20\%\text{--}40\%$). The target's own cash flows pay down the debt over a 3–7 year holding period.

### Sources of LBO Alpha:
1. **Debt Paydown (Deleveraging):** Cash flows amortize debt principal, transferring enterprise value from lenders to equity sponsors.
2. **Operational Expansion (EBITDA Growth):** Increasing revenues and cutting SG&A costs.
3. **Multiple Arbitrage:** Buying at $8\times \text{EV/EBITDA}$ and selling at $10\times$ upon exit.
