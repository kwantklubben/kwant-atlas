### 1. Intuition & Practical Objective

Traditional market data (prices, volumes, corporate earnings reports) is universally available: thousands of quantitative funds analyze the exact same price feeds simultaneously, driving alpha margins to near zero.

**Alternative Data** refers to non-traditional data exhaust generated by human activity, industrial processes, and digital commerce:
- Consumer credit card transaction panels
- Geolocation tracking of retail foot traffic
- Satellite imagery of oil storage tank shadows and retail parking lots
- Web-scraped product prices, inventory counts, and job postings

If an alternative dataset gives you visibility into a retailer's revenue 3 weeks before they publish official quarterly earnings, you hold a massive informational advantage.

---

### 2. Mathematical Ground Truth & Derivations

#### Point-in-Time (PIT) Database Architecture
The most common and lethal bug in alternative data research is **Point-in-Time violation**:
- Let real-world economic event occur at time $t_E$ (e.g., consumer makes credit card purchase on June 15).
- Let data vendor aggregate, clean, and deliver the record at time $t_A$ (e.g., delivery on June 22).
- If your backtest uses the record at $t \in [t_E, t_A)$, your backtest is trading on future information that was physically inaccessible at that moment!
Every alternative data table must have two distinct timestamp columns:
1. `event_timestamp` ($t_E$)
2. `knowledge_timestamp` ($t_K$, arrival into your database)
Signals must be computed strictly as a function of information where $t_K \le t_{\text{trade}}$.

#### Alpha Decay & Half-Life of Alternative Data
When an alternative dataset is proprietary to one fund, its Sharpe ratio might be $2.5$.
As the data vendor sells the same feed to 20 competing multi-manager hedge funds, alpha decays rapidly:
$$\text{Sharpe}(t) = \text{Sharpe}_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda_{\text{decay}} t}$$
High-frequency datasets decay in hours; quarterly alternative signals decay over 1 to 2 years.

### 3. Computational Implementation

```python
import pandas as pd

def enforce_point_in_time(transactions: pd.DataFrame, as_of_date: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Filters dataset to guarantee zero lookahead bias based on knowledge timestamp.
    """
    # Transactions schema: [transaction_date, ingestion_timestamp, merchant_id, amount]
    valid_records = transactions[transactions["ingestion_timestamp"] <= as_of_date]
    return valid_records
```

### 4. Failure Modes & First-Principles Breakdowns

1. **Panel Bias & Demographic Drift:**
   - *Failure:* A credit card data provider loses a major issuing bank partner, causing observed transactions to drop $30\%$ overnight.
   - *Symptom:* The quant model interprets data vendor attrition as a catastrophic retail collapse, shorting the stock right before blowout earnings.

2. **Regulatory & Compliance Violations (MNPI):**
   - *Failure:* Ingesting web-scraped data that violates terms of service or contains Material Non-Public Information (MNPI), exposing the fund to SEC insider trading prosecution.
