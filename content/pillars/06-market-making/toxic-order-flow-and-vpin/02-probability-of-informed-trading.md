### 1. Intuition & Practical Objective

PIN — the **probability of informed trading** — answers one question precisely: *what fraction of the order flow is informed?* The Glosten–Milgrom machinery ([[pillars/06-market-making/adverse-selection-and-glosten-milgrom/index|Adverse Selection & GM]]) needs the informed fraction $\pi$ to set the spread; PIN is the *empirical estimator* of that fraction from observable daily buy/sell counts.

The intuition is statistical and elegant: **informed traders distort the order flow to one side.** On days with no information event, buys and sells arrive in roughly equal (Poisson) numbers. On days with good news, informed traders pile onto the *buy* side; on bad-news days, onto the *sell* side. The total daily buy/sell counts therefore follow a **mixture** — a blend of a balanced Poisson flow (no event) and two lopsided Poisson flows (good/bad event). PIN is the share of the total flow that comes from the informed, one-sided component.

The practical objective: take daily buy/sell counts, fit the Poisson-mixture model by maximum likelihood, and recover PIN as a number in $[0,1]$ that a market maker can use to price the adverse-selection spread, and an asset-pricing researcher can use as an "information risk" factor.

---

### 2. Mathematical Ground Truth & Derivations

**Model (Easley–Kiefer–O'Hara 1997; Hasbrouck Ch 6).** On each day, with probability $\alpha$ an information event occurs. If it does, informed traders arrive as a Poisson process of intensity $\mu$, and — knowing the state — all trade on the *same* side. Uninformed traders arrive on both sides as Poisson processes of intensity $\epsilon$ each, every day.

**Arrival rates.** Over the sample, the informed component contributes $\alpha\mu$ orders; the uninformed component contributes $2\epsilon$ ($\epsilon$ buys + $\epsilon$ sells). **PIN is the informed fraction**:

$$
\boxed{\;\mathrm{PIN}=\frac{\alpha\mu}{\alpha\mu+2\epsilon}\;}\qquad(\text{Hasbrouck eq. 6.4}).
$$

**Why this is "the probability of informed trading."** Pick a random arriving order. With probability $\alpha\mu/(\alpha\mu+2\epsilon)$ it was generated by an informed trader; the complement by an uninformed one. So PIN literally is the probability that the *next* order is informed — the quantity the GM spread formula requires.

**Identification (the deep, testable fact).** Only the *product* $\alpha\mu$ is identified from the daily counts — the likelihood is nearly flat over combinations of $\alpha$ and $\mu$ that yield the same $\alpha\mu$. But because PIN depends only on $\alpha\mu$, PIN itself is **stable and estimable** even though $\alpha$ and $\mu$ individually are imprecise (Hasbrouck Ch 6).

**Asymptotics.** With $\mathrm{PIN}\to0$ as $\epsilon\to\infty$ (noise drowns the signal) or $\mu\to0$ (no informed traders); $\mathrm{PIN}\to1$ as informed flow swamps noise. PIN rises with the severity of adverse selection — exactly the toxicity this pillar is about.

### 3. Computational Implementation — PIN is the informed fraction (stdlib only)

The cleanest verification is direct: simulate order *arrivals* under the model and count what fraction come from informed traders — it must equal the formula.

```python
import math, random

def pin(alpha, mu, eps):
    return alpha * mu / (alpha * mu + 2 * eps)

print("PIN = alpha*mu / (alpha*mu + 2*eps)   -- the informed fraction of order flow")
for a, m, e in ((0.30, 10, 2), (0.30, 30, 2), (0.50, 5, 5), (0.0, 10, 2)):
    print(f"  alpha={a} mu={m} eps={e}:  PIN={pin(a, m, e):.4f}")

# Direct check: simulate arrivals and count the informed-originated fraction.
def simulate_order_flow(alpha, mu, eps, days, seed=7):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    informed = uninformed = 0
    for _ in range(days):
        event = rng.random() < alpha
        if event:
            informed += round(rng.gauss(mu, math.sqrt(mu)))     # Poisson(mu) on event day
        uninformed += round(rng.gauss(eps, math.sqrt(eps)))     # Poisson(eps) buys
        uninformed += round(rng.gauss(eps, math.sqrt(eps)))     # Poisson(eps) sells
    return informed, uninformed

a, m, e = 0.30, 10.0, 2.0
i, u = simulate_order_flow(a, m, e, 20000)
frac = i / (i + u)
print(f"\nSimulated {20000} days (alpha={a}, mu={m}, eps={e}):")
print(f"  informed-originated orders = {i},  total = {i + u}")
print(f"  informed fraction = {frac:.4f}   (theory PIN = {pin(a, m, e):.4f})")
```
```
PIN = alpha*mu / (alpha*mu + 2*eps)   -- the informed fraction of order flow
  alpha=0.3 mu=10 eps=2:  PIN=0.4286
  alpha=0.3 mu=30 eps=2:  PIN=0.6923
  alpha=0.5 mu=5 eps=5:  PIN=0.2000
  alpha=0.0 mu=10 eps=2:  PIN=0.0000

Simulated 20000 days (alpha=0.3, mu=10.0, eps=2.0):
  informed-originated orders = 60541,  total = 140504
  informed fraction = 0.4309   (theory PIN = 0.4286)
```

The simulated informed fraction (0.4309) converges to the formula (0.4286) — PIN really is the informed share of the flow. And note the *pattern*: heavier informed activity ($\mu{=}30$) pushes PIN from 0.43 to 0.69 (more toxic), while zero informed traders ($\alpha{=}0$) pins PIN at exactly 0.

### 4. Failure Modes & First-Principles Breakdowns

1. **Only $\alpha\mu$ is identified, not $\alpha$ and $\mu$ separately.** The MLE cannot cleanly separate "many rare events" from "few common events." Over-interpreting individual $\alpha$ or $\mu$ estimates is wrong; the *product* (hence PIN) is the stable object.
2. **Daily-count aggregation loses intraday information.** Only the daily total buy/sell count enters the likelihood — the sequence, the timing, and the size of trades are discarded (Hasbrouck Ch 6, §6.3). That's why the high-frequency successor **VPIN** moves to volume buckets ([[pillars/06-market-making/toxic-order-flow-and-vpin/04-vpin|04 · VPIN]]).
3. **"Informed" is defined by one-sidedness, not by actual news.** PIN detects *imbalanced* flow; a legitimate one-way flow (a large fund rebalancing, a short-squeeze covering wave) is indistinguishable from informed flow by counts alone.
