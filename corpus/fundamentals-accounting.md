# Fundamentals & Accounting — Corpus Wishlist

> **Area:** Fundamentals & Accounting (new cross-cutting area of the Kwant-Atlas).
> **Purpose:** Build depth and authority for the Fundamentals & Accounting knowledge area, which currently has **zero** canonical coverage in the Atlas. The member's own Notes & Learning (fundamentals, valuation, DCF, accounting, a working 5-workbook screening blueprint) are already embedded from personal notes; this wishlist is the **canonical textbook & paper layer** that gives the area rigor, replicable methods, and academic grounding.

## Scope & audience
Club members arrive from very different backgrounds — some have never seen a balance sheet, others already run quantamental screens. The curated spine below deliberately spans the full ladder: **from-zero accounting literacy** → **financial-statement analysis** → **valuation** → **screening/quantamental factors** → **fraud & red flags** → **the data that powers it**. Every sub-topic folder planned for this area maps to works here.

## financial-statements-accounting — "What *is* a balance sheet?"

The on-ramp. These books teach the three statements, the accounting equation, and how a reporting framework turns transactions into line items. Ratios and analysis come later.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Thomas R. Ittelson — *Financial Statements: A Step-by-Step Guide to Understanding and Creating Financial Reports*** (Career Press, 4th ed. 2020). Plain-English walk-through of balance sheet / income statement / cash-flow statement built line by line. The single best first book for a member who has never read statements.
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Darrell Mullis & Judith Orloff — *The Accounting Game: Basic Accounting Fresh from the Lemonade Stand*** (Career Press, 1998/revised). Teaches the accounting equation, double-entry, and cash vs. accrual using a running lemonade-stand case. Gentle, intuitive zero-point entry.
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Benjamin Graham & Spencer B. Meredith — *The Interpretation of Financial Statements*** (HarperBusiness reissue of 1937 original). Reads the *balance sheet, income statement, and ratios line by line through a value investor's lens.* The classic skeleton that the qualitative screening books (Security Analysis, Intelligent Investor) assume.
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Donald E. Kieso, Jerry J. Weygandt & Terry D. Warfield — *Intermediate Accounting*** (Wiley, 18th ed.). The US-GAAP reference of record for *how specific line items are measured* (leases, revenue recognition, pensions, deferred taxes). Use as the deep dictionary when a screen hits a measurement nuance — not as a cover-to-cover read.

> **Notes:** William R. Scott, *Financial Accounting Theory* (7th ed.) is the academic-theory counterpart (informational role of accounting, contracting, value relevance); a later, optional acquisition for members who want the *theory* layer. Financial-accounting catalogs and DuPont decomposition are taught rigorously inside the financial-statement-analysis books below, so no separate ratio-pedagogy text is needed at the zero level.

## financial-statement-analysis — turning statements into analysis

The analytical core of the whole area. These are the frameworks that convert raw statements into *decisions* (value, distress, quality, growth) rather than descriptions.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Stephen H. Penman — *Financial Statement Analysis and Security Valuation*** (McGraw-Hill, 5th ed. 2013). **The spine of this area.** Rebuilds valuation from accounting fundamentals — protection of book value, residual-income valuation, forecasting from the financial statements. Why analysts who ignore what the statements say about *value creation* misprice stocks. Pairs accounting accruals theory with the practical "business activities" approach. Some parts accessible on archive.org.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Stephen H. Penman — *Accounting for Value*** (Columbia University Press, 2010). The same accounting-to-value logic compressed into an investor manifesto: anchors on book value and earnings *growth with quality*, warns against speculative growth expectations baked into price. Excellent bridge from accounting into Graham-style value.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **K. R. Subramanyam — *Financial Statement Analysis*** (McGraw-Hill, 11th ed.). The classic academic FSA text: ratio systems, DuPont decomposition, cash-flow analysis, and earnings quality in one rigorous volume. Strong on the *mechanics* Penman then pushes further.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Krishna Palepu & Paul Healy — *Business Analysis and Valuation: Using Financial Statements*** (Cengage, 5th ed.). The Harvard Business School framework: **strategy → accounting → financial → prospective** analysis. The canonical "how a fundamental analyst actually interrogates a company" method; underpins the club's screening blueprint.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Martin Fridson & Fernando Alvarez — *Financial Statement Analysis: A Practitioner's Guide*** (Wiley, 4th ed. 2011). The practitioner's classic: ratio interpretation with realistic corporate context, credit and equity lenses, and honest warnings about when standard analysis breaks. Fills the gap between academic text and live decisions.

> **Optional later depth:** *Financial Statement Analysis & Valuation* (Easton, McAnally, Sommers) parallels Subramanyam for those who want the FASB-line-item treatment.

## core-financial-ratios — ratio frameworks that predict outcomes

Ratios are catalogs; the *canonical literature* here is the handful of frameworks that turned specific ratios into predictive instruments — distress and long-run valuation. This folder stays deliberately lean because ratio construction/interpretation is fully covered inside Penman, Subramanyam, Fridson & Alvarez, and Pinto (equity-valuation). What belongs here is the *predictive evidence*.

### Papers
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Edward I. Altman — "Financial Ratios, Discriminant Analysis and the Prediction of Corporate Bankruptcy"** (*JF*, 1968, 23(4), 589–609). The **Z-score**: a weighted ratio composite that separates bankrupt from healthy firms. The canonical demonstration that a handful of ratios carry real predictive power.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **James Ohlson — "Financial Ratios and the Probabilistic Prediction of Bankruptcy"** (*JAR*, 1980, 18(1), 109–131). The **O-score** logit model — the probabilistic, econometrically cleaner successor to Altman; standard in credit screens and distress research.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **John Y. Campbell & Robert J. Shiller — "Valuation Ratios and the Long-Run Stock Market Outlook"** (*Journal of Portfolio Management*, 1998, 24(2)). Shows **cash-flow-anchored ratios** (dividend/earnings yield) forecast long-run market returns — the ratio-to-return evidence that justifies cheapness screens.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Edward I. Altman — *Corporate Financial Distress and Bankruptcy* (3rd ed., Wiley, 2006).** The full treatment of ratio-based distress prediction and restructuring — the book-length companion to the Z-score papers.

## equity-valuation — DCF, relative / comps, and value logic

How balance-sheet and income-statement fundamentals become a *price*. DCF (enterprise and equity), residual income, and relative valuation (multiples/comps) — the quant layer under any screen.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Aswath Damodaran — *Investment Valuation: Tools and Techniques for Determining the Value of Any Asset*** (Wiley, 3rd ed. 2012). **The valuation canon** — every framework (DCF, multiples, LBO-flavored, real options) with the "when to use which" judgment. Companion data/tools freely maintained on Damodaran's NYU Stern page.
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Aswath Damodaran — *The Dark Side of Valuation: Valuing Young, Distressed, and Complex Businesses*** (Pearson, 2nd ed. 2018). How to value the hard cases the clean-DCF textbooks dodge — young growth firms, cyclical/distressed, financial firms — the precise situations a screen throws up.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Tim Koller, Marc Goedhart & David Wessels (McKinsey) — *Valuation: Measuring and Managing the Value of Companies*** (Wiley, 8th ed. 2020). The **enterprise-DCF** standard from the practitioner side: building a defensible DCF from fundamentals, ROIC and growth drivers, and error-checking the model. The framework most quantamental equity models mirror.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Jerald E. Pinto, Elaine Henry, Thomas R. Robinson & John D. Stowe (CFA Institute) — *Equity Asset Valuation*** (Wiley). The professional-standard treatment that explicitly covers **relative valuation / multiples (comps)** alongside DCF, residual income, and free cash flow. Best single source for the comps layer.
- `[Book]` 🟢 **John Burr Williams — *The Theory of Investment Value*** (Harvard University Press, 1938). The origin of the **dividend/discount and "intrinsic value = present value of future flows"** framework — historically foundational reading for how fundamentals map to worth.

> **Intro on-ramp (optional):** Damodaran, *The Little Book of Valuation* (2011) is a compact first read before the full text; both Damodaran valuation books share his NYU pages, so acquire one and reuse the other's data appendices.

## fundamental-analysis-and-screening — Graham school, quality, and structured screens

The discretionary side of fundamentals: what to look *for* in a business, and the classic value/quality screen templates this area turns into quantamental rules.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Benjamin Graham & David Dodd — *Security Analysis*** (McGraw-Hill, 6th ed. 2008, forewords by Warren Buffett and Seth Klarman). The founding text of fundamental equity analysis — margin of safety, earnings power, asset-value floors. The intellectual source of most value screens.
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Benjamin Graham — *The Intelligent Investor*** (rev. ed. 1973, HarperBusiness annotated 4th ed. 2003 with Jason Zweig). The readable distillation: margin of safety, Mr. Market, defensive vs. enterprising criteria. The behavioral/decision spine under the mechanical Graham screens (net-nets, low P/E, defensive screens).
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Philip A. Fisher — *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits and Other Writings*** (1958, Wiley reissue). The *growth/quality* counterweight to Graham — the "scuttlebutt" qualitative criteria (business quality, management, moat) that become the qualitative half of quality screens.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Bruce Greenwald, Judd Kahn, Paul Sonkin & Michael van Biema — *Value Investing: From Graham to Buffett and Beyond*** (Wiley, 2001). Formalizes value investing as asset-based, earnings-power, and franchise valuation — the structure that turns Graham's heuristics into an analyzable method.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Pat Dorsey (Morningstar) — *The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing*** (2004). The institutional-equity-research workflow (moats, financial health, valuation) that Morningstar turned into durable screens; strong on *economic moats* and on filtering a broad universe down to candidates.

> **Bridge note:** the *systematic* screen-evidence books (O'Shaughnessy, Greenblatt, Gray & Carlisle) are filed under **quantitative-fundamental-investing** below — read them alongside this folder, they are the quant translations of these discretionary classics.

## capital-structure-and-corporate-finance — financing, leverage, and the theory behind ratios

Why firms choose debt vs. equity, how that changes WACC and value, and the agency/tax forces behind capital structure — the corporate-finance layer that explains the leverage and coverage ratios in every financial statement.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Richard Brealey, Stewart Myers & Franklin Allen — *Principles of Corporate Finance*** (McGraw-Hill, 13th ed.). The canonical corporate-finance text — NPV, capital structure, payout policy, valuation. The theory backbone for the whole area.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Jonathan Berk & Peter DeMarzo — *Corporate Finance*** (Pearson, 5th ed.). The modern rigorous alternative to Brealey; exceptionally clear on leverage, WACC, and how financing affects the statements. Widely used in top programs.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Aswath Damodaran — *Applied Corporate Finance*** (Wiley, 4th ed.). Decisions not theory: investment, financing, and dividend decisions, with the estimation of cost of capital and sustainable debt ratios worked through real firms — the practitioner bridge from valuation to capital structure.
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Jean Tirole — *The Theory of Corporate Finance*** (Princeton University Press, 2006). The graduate-level theory — agency, contracting, liquidity, and governance models underlying capital-structure choices. Deep reference for members who want the model under the empirical patterns.
- `[Book]` 🔴 **Mark Grinblatt & Sheridan Titman — *Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy*** (McGraw-Hill, 2nd ed.). Unifies corporate finance with financial-markets theory (arbitrage, risk pricing) — how capital-structure decisions interact with market prices. Useful for quant members connecting corporate actions to return models.

### Papers
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Franco Modigliani & Merton H. Miller — "The Cost of Capital, Corporation Finance and the Theory of Investment"** (*AER*, 1958, 48(3), 261–297). The **M&M irrelevance** propositions — the null hypothesis every capital-structure and valuation question starts from.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Stewart C. Myers & Nicholas S. Majluf — "Corporate Financing and Investment Decisions When Firms Have Information That Investors Do Not Have"** (*JFE*, 1984, 13(2), 187–221). The **pecking-order** theory: information asymmetry drives financing choices and explains observed debt/equity patterns.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Michael C. Jensen & William H. Meckling — "Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure"** (*JFE*, 1976, 3(4), 305–360). **Agency costs** — the divergence between managers and owners that accounting quality, governance, and many red-flag signals exist to measure.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Michael C. Jensen — "Agency Costs of Free Cash Flow, Corporate Finance, and Takeovers"** (*AER*, 1986, 76(2), 323–329). Why **free cash flow** in low-growth firms invites value-destroying spending — the conceptual base for free-cash-flow quality screens and payout analysis.

## accounting-quality-and-red-flags — manipulation, accruals, and fraud detection

The defense layer: how reported earnings can be detached from economic reality, how to detect it, and the academic evidence that accrual-based manipulation is priced.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Howard M. Schilit, Jeremy Perler & Yoni Engelhart — *Financial Shenanigans: How to Detect Accounting Gimmicks and Fraud in Financial Reports*** (McGraw-Hill, 4th ed. 2020). **The detection bible** — a taxonomized catalog of earnings/CF/balance-sheet shenanigans with real cases and detection signals. Required for the "red flags" folder.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Charles W. Mulford & Eugene E. Comiskey — *The Financial Numbers Game: Detecting Creative Accounting Practices*** (Wiley, 2002). The academic-accountant's rigorous companion to Schilit — how aggressive accounting choices bend earnings, and how to reverse-engineer them from the statements.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Thornton L. O'Glove — *Quality of Earnings*** (Free Press, 1987). The cult classic that reframes the whole discipline around **free cash flow vs. reported earnings** — the original case for cash-flow-based earnings-quality analysis.

### Papers
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Richard G. Sloan — "Do Stock Prices Fully Reflect Information in Accruals and Cash Flows About Future Earnings?"** (*TAR*, 1996, 71(3), 289–315). The **accruals anomaly**: high-accrual (low cash-flow) firms underperform. Foundational — accruals quality as a priced signal and the seed of the "accruals" quality factor.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Patricia M. Dechow, Richard G. Sloan & Amy P. Sweeney — "Detecting Earnings Management"** (*TAR*, 1995, 70(2), 193–225). The **modified-Jones model** of discretionary accruals — the standard econometric tool for detecting earnings management in a panel.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Messod D. Beneish — "The Detection of Earnings Manipulation"** (*FAJ*, 1999, 55(5), 24–36). The **M-score** — a multi-variable screen for likely manipulators; widely used as the quantitative red-flag.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Paul M. Healy & James M. Wahlen — "A Review of the Earnings Management Literature and Its Implications for Standard Setting"** (*Accounting Horizons*, 1999, 13(4), 365–383). The organizing survey of why and how earnings get managed — the theory under every red-flag heuristic.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Patricia Dechow, Weili Ge & Catherine Schrand — "Understanding Earnings Quality: A Review of the Proxies, Their Determinants and Their Consequences"** (*JAE*, 2010, 50(2–3), 344–401). The definitive map of every earnings-quality proxy and what each does — essential before choosing a "quality" metric for a screen.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Louis K. C. Chan, Narasimhan Jegadeesh & Josef Lakonishok — "Earnings Quality and Stock Returns"** (*JF*, 2006, 61(2), 769–806). Shows earnings-quality measures (accruals, cash-flow persistence) carry return-predictive power beyond value — the bridge from red flags to factors.

## quantitative-fundamental-investing — fundamental factors & evidence

The systematic layer: which *fundamental* characteristics historically earned a premium, and the factor structure (Fama–French and successors) that quantamental screens actually operationalize. Companion theory lives in pillar 01 (Quantitative Research) of the Atlas.

### Papers
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Ray Ball & Philip Brown — "An Empirical Evaluation of Accounting Income Numbers"** (*JAR*, 1968, 6(2), 159–178). The origin of the finding that accounting earnings move stock prices — the empirical foundation of the whole fundamental-information line of research.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Eugene F. Fama & Kenneth R. French — "The Cross-Section of Expected Stock Returns"** (*JF*, 1992, 47(2), 427–465). The paper that killed the simple CAPM story and established **size + book-to-market (value)** as cross-sectional return drivers.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Eugene F. Fama & Kenneth R. French — "Common Risk Factors in the Returns on Stocks and Bonds"** (*JFE*, 1993, 33(1), 3–56). The canonical **three-factor model** (market, SMB size, HML value).
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Eugene F. Fama & Kenneth R. French — "A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model"** (*JFE*, 2015, 116(1), 1–22). Adds **profitability (RMW)** and **investment (CMA)** — directly tying *accounting fundamentals* (gross profitability, asset growth) into the factor model. This is the official seat of fundamentals in asset pricing.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Robert Novy-Marx — "The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability Premium"** (*JFE*, 2013, 108(1), 1–28). **Gross profitability** as a powerful return predictor orthogonal to value — the cleanest single profitability signal and a keystone of quantamental screens.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Kewei Hou, Chen Xue & Lu Zhang — "Digesting Anomalies: An Investment Approach"** (*RFS*, 2015, 28(3), 650–705). The **q-factor model** (investment + ROE) that "digests" most anomalies through a production-based investment lens — the theory unifying valuation, investment, and profitability.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Joseph D. Piotroski — "Value Investing: The Use of Historical Financial Statement Information to Separate Winners from Losers"** (*JAR*, 2000, 38(Supplement), 1–41). The **F-score**: a composite of 9 fundamental signals that boosts value-strategy returns — the most-cited bridge from financial-statement analysis to a mechanical screen.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Jeremiah Green, John R. M. Hand & X. Frank Zhang — "The Characteristics That Provide Independent Information About Average U.S. Monthly Stock Returns"** (*RFS*, 2017, 30(12), 4389–4436). The comprehensive study of **94 firm characteristics** — which are genuinely informative and which are redundant. The empirical map for anyone building a many-variable quantamental screen.
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Victor L. Bernard & Jacob K. Thomas — "Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium?"** (*JAR*, 1989, 27(Supplement), 1–36). The **PEAD** anomaly — prices drift after earnings surprises because the market underreacts to the *implications* of earnings for future earnings. Fundamental information and its slow digestion.

### Books
- `[Book]` 🟢 **Joel Greenblatt — *The Little Book That Beats the Market*** (Wiley, 2005; *Revisited* 2010). The **magic formula** — rank on ROIC + earnings yield, buy the top. The most famous simple quantamental screen in print; the club's 5-workbook system generalizes exactly this logic.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **Wesley R. Gray & Tobias E. Carlisle — *Quantitative Value: A Practitioner's Guide to Automating Intelligent Investment and Eliminating Behavioral Errors*** (Wiley, 2013). Combines value, quality, and earnings-quality signals into a rigorous quantamental screen, with full backtest transparency. The closest published cousin to the club's screening blueprint.
- `[Book]` 🟡 **James P. O'Shaughnessy — *What Works on Wall Street: The Classic Guide to the Best-Performing Investment Strategies*** (McGraw-Hill, 4th ed.). 80+ years of systematic factor/screen backtests (value, quality, growth, size) with turnover and drawdown honesty — the evidence base for which single-factor screens endure.

## data-sources-and-corporate-data — where the numbers come from

Fundamental analysis lives or dies on *data quality and timing*. This folder is intentionally a **data/reference** list rather than a reading list: point-in-time hygiene, look-ahead/survivorship bias, and machine-readable filings are what let a quantamental screen be *built* rather than only described.

### Data sources
- `[Data]` `[Free]` 🟢 **SEC EDGAR** — company *filings, XBRL financial statements, and structured APIs* (companyfacts, full-text search). Free, machine-readable fundamentals for US-listed companies. The club's primary raw input.
- `[Data]` 🟡 **WRDS — Compustat (+ CRSP linkage)** — the institutional fundamental database; standardized annual/quarterly financials going back decades, with the CRSP return link used in every academic factor study.
- `[Data]` 🔴 **Compustat Point-in-Time (WRDS PIT)** — restated/revised history as it was *originally reported*, eliminating look-ahead and survivorship bias. Critical hygiene for backtesting screens; naive "as-filed-latest" Compustat inflates results.
- `[Data]` `[Free]` 🟢 **Kenneth R. French Data Library** — the canonical factor return series (SMB/HML/RMW/CMA and portfolios), free, enabling the club to *replicate and validate* Fama–French results.
- `[Data]` `[Free]` 🟡 **XBRL US-GAAP / IFRS taxonomies** — the tagged-line-item dictionaries behind EDGAR XBRL; understanding tags is what makes programmatic financial extraction reliable.

### Papers / references
- `[Paper]` 🟡 **Tim Loughran & Bill McDonald — "When Is a Liability Not a Liability? Textual Analysis, Dictionaries, and 10-Ks"** (*JF*, 2011, 66(1), 35–65). The standard method/dictionary for mining 10-K text (tone, risk factors, disclosures) — the canonical link between *filings data* and *quantitative textual signals*, and the underpinning of the Atlas's 10-K-delta NLP topic.
- `[Paper]` 🔴 **Point-in-time methodology literature** (see WRDS PIT documentation and Compustat user guides) — not a single canonical paper, but the sourcing hygiene every fundamentals backtest must obey; cross-reference the Atlas pillar on backtesting hygiene for bias accounting.
