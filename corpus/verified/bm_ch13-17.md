## Chapter 13 — Pricing Derivatives on a Single Interest-Rate Curve (p547)
**Extraction verdict:** ✅ accurate, slightly shallow on technique detail. Corrections: minor.

**Structure (13.1–13.16)** — verified against ToC lines 2011–2054 and body:
13.1 In-Arrears Swaps · 13.2 In-Arrears Caps (13.2.1 LFM formula, 13.2.2 G2++ formula) · 13.3 Autocaps · 13.4 Caps with Deferred Caplets (LFM & G2++ versions) · 13.5 Ratchet Caps and Floors (LFM analytical approximation) · 13.6 Ratchets / One-Way Floaters · 13.7 Constant-Maturity Swaps (CMS) (LFM and G2++) · 13.8 The Convexity Adjustment & Applications to CMS (13.8.1 natural vs unnatural time lags; 13.8.2 technique; 13.8.4 application to CMS; 13.8.5 unnatural resetting & average-rate swaps) · 13.9 Average Rate Caps · 13.10 Captions and Floortions · 13.11 Zero-Coupon Swaptions · 13.12 Eurodollar Futures (13.12.1 G2++; 13.12.2 LFM) · 13.13 LFM Pricing with "In-Between" Spot Rates (13.13.1 Accrual swaps; 13.13.2 Trigger swaps) · 13.14 LFM pricing with early exercise & path dependence · 13.15 LFM: Bermudan Swaptions (13.15.1 LSMC; 13.15.2 Carr & Yang; 13.15.3 Andersen; 13.15.4 numerical example) · 13.16 New Generation of Contracts (13.16.1 TARNs; 13.16.2 CMS spread options).

**Verified key content the extract should state more explicitly:**
- In-arrears swaps/caps are priced by changing from `D(0,T_i)` discounting to the identity `E[X D(0,T)] = E[X D(0,S)/P(T,S)]` (eq 13.2) so each payoff term is valued under its own forward measure `Q^{i+1}`. In-arrears **swap** price (13.3) depends only on caplet volatilities `v_i` (from Black-inverted cap vols) — additive, one-rate-per-time → **correlation-free**; price depends on vol only through `F_{i+1}(0)^2 e^{v_{i+1}^2}`. Remark 13.1.1 makes it smile-consistent via `E[F^2] = 2∫(F−K)^+ dK`, so the value can be written from *market caplet prices* — a good point to add.
- In-arrears **cap** (13.2.1, LFM): `Σ τ_{i+1} P(0,T_{i+1}) [Bl(K,F_{i+1}(0),v_{i+1}) + τ_{i+1} g(K,F_{i+1}(0),v_{i+1})]` with explicit `g` (a "second-order" lognormal term). G2++ counterpart in 13.2.2 exploits lognormal bond prices.
- **Autocaps** (13.3): cap where each caplet's strike = previous caplet's fixing.
- **Natural vs unnatural time lag (13.8.1):** natural lag (pay at the rate's own maturity `T_{i+1}`) ⇒ leg value `P(0,T_α)−P(0,T_β)`, **volatility-independent**; unnatural lag (pay at reset date, in-arrears) ⇒ volatility-dependent. This is the core CMS/convexity-adjustment insight the extract gestures at but should name precisely.
- **Convexity adjustment (13.8.2):** for a payment of a swap rate `τ_i S_{α,β}(T_{i−k})` due at `T_i` (unnatural lag), correct the forward swap rate; CMS is the `i=α, k=1 or 0` case. Worked "natural vs unnatural" FRA results: forward (natural) FRA values at `P(0,T_1)−P(0,T_2)`; in-arrears FRA gains `+P(0,T_2)τ^2 F_2(0)^2 v_2^2(T_1)` (eq 13.12).
- **Average-rate caps (13.9):** average of ~30 daily six-month-LIBOR fixings over the month before `T_i`, paid `T_{i+1}`; priced analytically via **basket-option approximation** (subset simulation an alternative).
- **Zero-coupon swaptions (13.11):** a zero-coupon IRS (single fixed final payment `τ_{α,β}K P(T_α,T_β)` vs usual floating leg) reduces, in the one-payment case, to a forward rate; option then collapses toward a standard swaption on a forward rate. (Extract's parenthetical "zero-coupon swaptions" is correct.)
- **Eurodollar futures (13.12):** payoff `X(1−L(S_1,S_2))`; the futures expectation `E_t[1/P(S_1,S_2)]` must be taken under the **risk-neutral** measure (daily settlement) — model-dependent, unlike the forward measure which would give `X(1−F(0;S_1,S_2))`. Solved in G2++ and LFM.
- **Accrual & trigger swaps (13.13):** "in-between" spot rates needed because the accrual/trigger decisions happen at dates not on the LFM tenor grid — handled by LFM pricing with in-between spot rates.
- **Bermudan swaptions (13.15):** option to enter an IRS at any reset date `T_l ∈ [T_k,T_h]` into a swap whose start & length depend on exercise time (Def 13.15.1). Three LFM methods: (i) **LSMC** (Longstaff–Schwartz) — regress continuation values on basis functions; (ii) **Carr & Yang (1997)** — Markov-chain approximation from simulation, price via backward induction; (iii) **Andersen (1999)** — approximate exercise boundary / curve approach. Numerical example compares them (13.15.4).
- **New-generation contracts (13.16):** **TARN** — coupon-bearing note, coupons subject to a trigger (`ωL≤ωX`), auto-expires once cumulative coupons hit target `S` (may skip payments / mature early; structure given by the `C(T_i)` recurrence, eq. 13.26–13.29); priced in the LFM. **CMS spread options** (13.16.2) — option `(ω[S_1(T)−S_2(T)]−...−K)^+` on the spread of two swap rates, valued with the individual CMS convexity adjustments (13.16) plus a **correlation parameter**; callable CMS-spread variants noted.

**Corrections/gaps to record:** extract is essentially right; add (i) the forward-measure change-of-discount identity (13.2) as the unifying technique; (ii) the "natural vs unnatural lag ⇒ vol dependence/independence" definition (this *is* what convexity adjustment means and the extract's "natural vs unnatural time lags" is vague); (iii) that in-arrears/CMS-like payoffs are correlation-free in their own rate only, but CMS-spread products require correlation; (iv) name the three Bermudan LFM methods (extract lists LSMC/Carr–Yang/Andersen — confirmed correct).

## Chapter 14 — Pricing Derivatives on Two Interest-Rate Curves (p607)
**Extraction verdict:** ✅ accurate. Minor additions below.

**Structure (14.1–14.5)** — verified ToC lines 2056–2080:
14.1 G2++ for multi-curve payoffs (14.1.1 the model; 14.1.2 interaction between two curves; 14.1.3 two-model dynamics under one forward measure) · 14.2 Quanto CMS (14.2.1 contract; 14.2.2 G2++ valuation; 14.2.3 **quanto adjustment**) · 14.3 Differential swaps (14.3.1 contract; 14.3.2 G2++; 14.3.3 market-like formula) · 14.4 Market formulas for basic quanto derivatives (14.4.1 quanto caplets/floorlets; 14.4.2 quanto caps/floors; 14.4.3 differential swaps; 14.4.4 quanto swaptions) · 14.5 Options on two-currency LIBOR rates (14.5.1 spread options; 14.5.2 options on the product; 14.5.3 trigger swaps; 14.5.4 multiple dates).

**Verified key content:**
- Two curves = two independent **G2++** short-rate models `r_1 = x_1+y_1+φ_1` (Euro, curve "1", eq 14.1–14.3) and `r_2 = x_2+y_2+φ_2` (GBP, "2"), each Gaussian with conditional mean/variance as in (14.3). Correlation between curves enters only through correlation between the two short-rate *factors* (equivalently between their Brownian drivers); φ chosen to fit each market curve. Framework later rederives the two-model dynamics under a single convenient forward measure (14.1.3).
- **Quanto CMS (14.2):** CMS rate in a foreign currency paid in domestic currency; G2++ value computed via MC of the joint domestic-foreign factor model; **quanto adjustment (14.2.3):** moving foreign swap-rate dynamics from foreign risk-neutral `Q_2` to domestic `Q` via the numeraire `X(t)×(domestic bank account)` adds a drift term `+σ^{(2)}(t)νc` (exchange-rate volatility × swap-rate vol × correlation `c`) — the classic quanto correction.
- **Differential swaps (14.3):** floating leg pays a *foreign* LIBOR but in *domestic* currency; market-like formula obtained.
- **Market formulas (14.4):** quanto caplet/floorlet, quanto cap/floor, differential swap, quanto swaption — closed forms analogous to Black (extract correct).
- **Options on two LIBORs (14.5):** spread options and options on the *product* of two-currency forward LIBORs under an LFM-type market model with FX (Schlögl-type), and trigger swaps with payments in domestic currency triggered by either rate (14.5.3). **Schlögl (2002) consistency (14.5.4):** `FX(T_i)/FX(T_{i−1}) = (1+τ_i F_i)/(1+τ_i F_i^f)` (eq 14.54) means the FX and forward-rate volatilities cannot all be independent positive constants across consecutive maturities; resolved by *freezing forward rates at time 0* to get approximately constant admissible volatilities. This mirrors the identical Schögl constraint in the inflation chapter (16.25) — a nice cross-link.

**Corrections/gaps:** none material; add that the "two curves" are two **G2++** models correlated only through factor correlation, and name Schlögl's no-arbitrage volatility constraint (14.54) as the technical subtlety in multi-maturity quanto/spread pricing.

## Chapter 15 — Pricing of Inflation-Indexed Derivatives (p643)
**Extraction verdict:** the extract folds 15–20 into one block; it correctly names the JY model, ZCIIS/YYIIS, caplets and market models, but **blurs chapter boundaries and omits chapter-level specifics** given below. Needs correction for per-chapter granularity.

**Structure (15.1–15.3):** 15.1 The Foreign-Currency Analogy · 15.2 Definitions and Notation · 15.3 The JY Model.

**Verified content:**
- **Setup/motivation:** inflation = percentage increments of the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**; negative inflation possible so coupons are typically floored at zero (implicit zero-strike floor); **zero/low-strike floors are the most actively traded** inflation options; two main swap forms (annual / single payment at maturity).
- **15.1 Foreign-currency analogy:** real rates = interest rates of the real ("foreign") economy; CPI `I(t)` = the exchange rate between nominal (domestic) and real ("foreign") economies. One nominal unit at T worth `I(0)/I(T)` real units (with `I(0)=1`); pricing an inflation payoff ⇔ pricing a cross-currency derivative. Motivated purely by the CPI basket definition (no redundancy in modeling nominal rate + real rate + index).
- **15.2 Notation:** `P_n,P_r` nominal/real bond prices; forward LIBOR `F_x(t;T_{i−1},T_i)`, instantaneous forward `f_x`, short rates `n(t)=f_n(t,t)`, `r(t)=f_r(t,t)`; measures `Q_n,Q_r`; **forward CPI** `I_i(t) := I(t) P_r(t,T_i)/P_n(t,T_i)` (eq 15.1) — martingale under the nominal forward measure `Q_n^{T_i}` (the linchpin of all the market-model results).
- **15.3 JY model (Jarrow & Yildirim 2003):** one-factor **Gaussian HJM** for *both* nominal and real instantaneous forward rates with exponential vol `ς_n=σ_n e^{−a_n(T−t)}`, `ς_r=σ_r e^{−a_r(T−t)}` plus lognormal CPI `dI = Iµdt + σ_I I dW_I`, three Brownian drivers with correlations `ρ_{n,r},ρ_{n,I},ρ_{r,I}`. **Prop 15.3.1:** under `Q_n`, `dn=[ϑ_n−a_n n]dt+σ_n dW_n`; `dr=[ϑ_r − ρ_{r,I}σ_I σ_r − a_r r]dt+σ_r dW_r`; `dI = I[n−r]dt+σ_I I dW_I`. ϑ fitted to both curves (`ϑ_x` = usual HJM-consistent exact-fit function). Corollary: real short rate is still OU under `Q_n`; CPI `I(T)` is **lognormal under Q_n** (eq 15.3) — the property that makes ch17 caplet formulas closed-form.

**Corrections/gaps:** the existing extract should (a) state ch15 is the definitions/model chapter and separate it from ch16/17; (b) give the forward-CPI definition (15.1) as the organizing object; (c) note the JY model = two Gaussian one-factor HJM (nominal & real) + lognormal CPI with 3 correlated drivers, and the exact-fit ϑ functions.

## Chapter 16 — Inflation-Indexed Swaps (p649)
**Extraction verdict:** ⚠️ present only inside the folded 15–20 block; needs a proper separate entry (this is a self-contained chapter with three competing pricing approaches).

**Structure (16.1–16.5):** 16.1 Pricing of a ZCIIS · 16.2 Pricing of a YYIIS · 16.3 …with the JY model · 16.4 …with a First (LFM) Market Model · 16.5 …with a Second Market Model.

**Verified content:**
- **Products:** ZCIIS (zero-coupon inflation-indexed swap): fixed leg `N[(1+K)^M−1]` at `T_M` vs floating `N[I(T_M)/I_0 −1]`. YYIIS (year-on-year): at each `T_i` fixed `Nϕ_i K` vs floating `Nψ_i[I(T_i)/I(T_{i−1})−1]`.
- **ZCIIS pricing (16.1) — model-INDEPENDENT:** using the foreign-currency identity `I(t)P_r(t,T)=E_n^Q[..]=E^Q[e^{−∫n}I(T)]` (16.5), `ZCIIS(t)=N[(I(t)/I_0) P_r(t,T_M) − P_n(t,T_M)]`, at 0: `N[P_r(0,T_M)−P_n(0,T_M)]` (16.6–16.7). Pure no-arbitrage, no model. ⇒ **strip real zero-coupon prices from ZCIIS quotes:** `P_r(0,T_M) = P_n(0,T_M)(1+K(T_M))^M` (16.8). Forward CPI from quotes `I_M(0)=I(0)(1+K)^M` (Kazziha). This is a headline result worth surfacing in the Atlas.
- **YYIIS general reduction (16.2):** each floating leg = `ZCIIS(T_{i−1},T_i,…)`; inner expectation `E[e^{−∫n}P_r(T_{i−1},T_i)]` is model-dependent (a real bond's price under a nominal measure) — *not* the simple forward-price unless real rates are deterministic.
- **16.3 JY closed form:** value `Nψ_i P_n(t,T_{i−1})[P_r(t,T_i)/P_r(t,T_{i−1})] e^{C(t,T_{i−1},T_i)} − Nψ_i P_n(t,T_i)`, with explicit correction `C` (eq 16.15) depending on `σ_r,σ_n,σ_I`, `ρ_{r,I},ρ_{n,r}`; correction vanishes at `σ_r=0` (deterministic real rates). Whole-leg price (16.16/16.17) as sum; final closed form in forward-rate ratios `(1+τ_i F_n)/(1+τ_i F_r)`.
- **16.4 First market model = Mercurio's LFM for both nominal and real rates** (analogous to cross-currency): forward CPI lognormal `dI_i=σ_{I,i}I_i dW_i^I` under `Q_n^{T_i}`; nominal forward `dF_n=σ_{n,i}F_n dW_n` (martingale), real forward has **drift** `dF_r=F_r(−ρ_{I,r,i}σ_{I,i}σ_{r,i}dt+σ_{r,i}dW_r)` (16.20). YYIIS price = one-dimensional integral over a bivariate-normal density of the log-ratios (16.24–16.27). Needs the CPI-vol freezing trick (Schlögl-type consistency (16.25)) so consecutive `σ_{I,i}` can be treated constant.
- **16.5 Second market model (Kazziha 1999; Belgrade–Benhamou–Koehler 2004; Mercurio 2005) = market model on forward CPI's only:** uses `I_i/I_{i−1}`, freezes the nominal-rate drift in `dI_{i−1}` under `Q_n^{T_i}` (16.29), giving a **fully analytical** price `Nψ_i P_n(t,T_i)[I_i(t)/I_{i-1}(t)]e^{D_i(t)}−…` with explicit correction `D_i` (16.30–16.32); key advantage — **`D` does not depend on real-rate volatility** (only CPI vols + nominal-rate vols/correlations), which are hard to calibrate. Drawback: freezing approximation may be rough for long `T_i` (exact only if `ρ_{I,n,i}=0`).

**Corrections/gaps:** the extract must recognize ch16 as its own chapter on **inflation-indexed SWAPS**, and distinguish the *three* YYIIS pricing routes (JY closed form with correction C; Mercurio LFM "first market model" → numerical integral; forward-CPI "second market model" → fully analytical with D). Also surface the **model-independent ZCIIS → real-curve stripping** (16.8).

## Chapter 17 — Inflation-Indexed Caplets/Floorlets (p661)
**Extraction verdict:** ⚠️ present only inside the folded 15–20 block; needs a proper separate entry.

**Structure (17.1–17.3 + chapter Appendix):** 17.1 Pricing with the JY model · 17.2 Pricing with the Second Market Model · 17.3 Inflation-Indexed Caps (+ Appendix: IICapFloor pricing with the LFM).

**Verified content:**
- **Products:** Inflation-indexed caplet/floorlet (IICF) at `T_i`: `Nψ_i[ω(I(T_i)/I(T_{i−1})−1−κ)]^+`, `K:=1+κ`; pricing = a **forward-start (cliquet)** option on the CPI ratio (eq 17.1–17.2). IICap/IIFloor = a stream of these (17.8).
- **17.1 JY closed form:** because CPI is lognormal under `Q_n^{T_i}` (from ch15), `I(T_i)/I(T_{i−1})` is lognormal; the price is a Black-type formula (17.4): uses the mean from the YYIIS result (`E = [P_n(t,T_{i−1})P_r(t,T_i)/(P_n(t,T_i)P_r(t,T_{i−1}))] e^{C}`) and an explicit log-variance `V^2(t,T_{i−1},T_i)` (very long closed expression in `σ_n,σ_r,σ_I,a_n,a_r` and the three correlations).
- **17.2 Second market model:** tower property → forward-CPI setup; freezing again gives `ln[I_i/I_{i−1}]` Gaussian with variance `V_i(t)^2`; final closed Black-like formula (17.7) built on `(1+τ_i F_n)/(1+τ_i F_r) e^{D_i}` and `V_i`; authors stress the **Black–Scholes analogy** of (17.7) as practical support for modeling forward CPI's as geometric Brownian motions.
- **17.3 IICap/Floor:** sum of caplets, closed form (17.9) under the second market model. **Chapter Appendix** prices an IICap under the LFM: reduces dimensionality by assuming **deterministic real rates**, yielding an integral (17.13) over the lognormal nominal forward (extract should not claim the market-model caplet pricing is uniformly closed-form — the LFM variant needs a 1-D numerical integral).

**Corrections/gaps:** extract should state that inflation *caplets/floorlets are priced under both the JY model and the forward-CPI "second market model"* as closed Black-type formulas (17.4, 17.7) thanks to CPI lognormality, plus an LFM integral variant (appendix, deterministic-real-rates assumption). Note explicitly the distinction the book draws: ch17 handles options while ch16 handled swaps.
