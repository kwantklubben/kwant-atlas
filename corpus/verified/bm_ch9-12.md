## Ch 9 — Including the Smile in the LFM  (source §9.1–9.2)

### Verified content (from the body, not the preface)
- **Setup (9.1 Mini-tour):** a `T2`-maturity caplet resetting at `T1`, notional 1, pays
  `τ (F(T1;T1,T2) − K)^+` at `T2`; value `P(0,T2)τ E_0^{T2}[(F(T1;T1,T2)−K)^+]`. Under the LFM the
  rate is lognormal, `dF(t;T1,T2)=σ2(t)F dW_t` (eq 9.1), giving **Black's formula**
  `Cpl^Black(0,T1,T2,K)=P(0,T2)τ Bl(K,F2(0),v2(T1))`, `v2^2(T1)=∫σ2^2 dt`. Because LFM volatility is
  a property of the *rate*, not the *strike*, a single `v2(T1)` cannot match two market strikes:
  **market caplet prices require a strike-dependent `v^{MKT}(T1,K)`** → the curve
  `K ↦ v^{MKT}(T1,K)/√T1` is the **volatility smile** (smile = min near ATM underlying; skew = low
  strikes priced with higher vol than high strikes, footnote 1 also allows a slope-negative-at-ATM
  skew).
- **Implied distribution (Breeden–Litzenberger):** from interpolated prices,
  `Cpl^{MKT}=P(0,T2)τ∫(x−K)^+ p2(x)dx` and
  `∂²Cpl^{MKT}(0,T1,T2,K)/∂K² = P(0,T2)τ p2(K)` (eq 9.4). Under consistent lognormality `p2` would be
  lognormal; real markets imply a non-lognormal `p2`. The extraction problem is **under-determined**:
  only a finite set of strikes is quoted, and `p2` depends on the interpolation scheme (via the 2nd
  derivative).
- **Answering via dynamics (9.2):** an alternative driftless dynamics `dF=ν(t,F)dW` under the forward
  measure (eq 9.5). Deterministic `ν` → **local-volatility model** (e.g. `ν=σ2(t)F^γ`, `0≤γ≤1`);
  stochastic `ν` (e.g. `ν=ξ(t)F` with its own SDE) → **stochastic-volatility model**. The model smile
  is generated by re-inverting Black's formula strike-by-strike; `ν(·,F)` is tuned to match market
  vols. One must fit an entire implied-**volatility surface** (one curve per expiry).
- **Model taxonomy (§9.2, "Modeling the Smile"):** six families that can fit non-flat cap
  vol surfaces:
  1. **Local-volatility models (LVMs)** — CEV (Cox 1975; Cox–Ross 1976; LIBOR application
     Andersen–Andreasen 2000), displaced diffusion (Rubinstein 1983); smile-capable flexible LVMs
     by Brigo–Mercurio–Sartorelli 2003 / Brigo–Mercurio 2003.
  2. **Stochastic-volatility models (SVMs)** — Hull–White 1987, Heston 1993 (LMM versions Wu–Zhang
     2002); Andersen–Brotherton-Ratcliffe 2001, HKLW 2002, Piterbarg 2003, Joshi–Rebonato 2003.
  3. **Jump-diffusion models (JDMs)** — Merton 1976 first; jump-diffusion LIBOR Glasserman–Merener
     2001, Glasserman–Kou 2003. Suited to pronounced short-maturity smiles/skews, but rarely used in
     rates (implementation difficulty + few short quotes).
  4. **Market models of implied volatility (MMsIV)** — Schönbucher 1999, Ledoit–Santa-Clara 1998;
     LMM version Brace–Goldys–Klebaner–Womersley 2001. Hard: implied vol drift is not a traded
     price, must impose no-arbitrage on option prices.
  5. **Levy-driven models (LDMs)** — Madan–Seneta 1990, Eberlein–Keller 1995; LMM/SMM versions
     Eberlein–Özkan 2005, Linev–Eberlein 2004. Good empirically (equity) but hard to calibrate/price
     exotics.
  6. **Uncertain-parameter models (UPMs)** — volatility a random *variable* drawn at an infinitesimal
     time (i.e. simplest SVM); Mercurio 2002, Alexander–Brintalos–Nogueira 2003, Gatarek 2003,
     Brigo–Mercurio–Rapisarda 2004; LFM extensions Gatarek 2003, Errais–Mauri–Mercurio 2004.
- The book only reviews **LVMs (Ch10), SVMs (Ch11), UPMs (Ch12)** — chosen for tractability /
  popularity; JDMs, MMsIV, LDMs are deliberately *not* reviewed (too hard to apply in the rates
  market).

## Ch 10 — Local-Volatility Models (for forward rates)

### Verified content and structure (body order differs from the extraction's list order)
Sections in source order: `10.1` shifted-lognormal; `10.2` CEV; `10.3` analytically-tractable class
(density mixtures); `10.4` LM; `10.5` forward-rate dynamics under different measures + `10.5.1`
decorrelation; `10.6` shifting LM; `10.7` LMDM; `10.8` HSDM; `10.9` market-data tests; `10.10`
second general class (function-of-Brownian); `10.11` mixture of GBMs; `10.12` GBM-mixture extension
for skews; `10.13` Dupire-à-la dynamics. **Chapter framing:** LVMs here are *calibrated to caps and
only used for LIBOR-dependent derivatives*; swaption/joint calibration is explicitly deferred to
SVMs (Ch11) and UPMs (Ch12).

1. **10.1 Shifted-lognormal** — `F_j=X_j+α`, `dF_j=β(t)(F_j−α)dW` (eq 10.2), shift-constant density;
   caplet `Cpl=τ N P(t,T_j) Bl(K−α, F_j(t)−α, U(t,T_{j−1}))` (eq 10.6) — closed form, produces only
   **skews** (α<0 decreasing & upward curve, α>0 increasing & downward). Too rigid (no strong negative
   slopes) and negative α puts mass on negative rates.
2. **10.2 CEV (Andersen–Andreasen 2000)** — `dF_j=σ_j(t)[F_j]^γ dW` (eq 10.7), `0<γ<1`, **absorbing
   boundary at 0 when γ<1/2**; via deterministic time change reduces to a Bessel-type process with
   explicit transition density (eq 10.9, modified Bessel `I_q`), zero-absorption probability a
   complementary-gamma `G(1/(2(1−γ)),u)`. **Caplet closed form in non-central chi-squared** (eqs
   10.10–10.11); γ>1 extension possible but explodes off the forward measure. **LCEV ("Limited" CEV)**
   fixes absorption: `φ(F)=F·min(ε^{γ−1},F^{γ−1})`. Approximate swaption prices via freezing + weight
   collapse.
3. **10.3 Analytically-tractable class (Brigo–Mercurio 2000b/2001b/2002b)** — choose local vol so the
   forward-rate density is a convex mixture `p_t=Σλ_i p^i_t` (eq 10.16) of densities of N driftless
   reference diffusions `G_i` (all starting at `F_j(0)`). Local vol from the Fokker–Planck equations:
   `σ²(t,y)=ΣΛ_i v_i²/y²` (eqs 10.20–10.22) — the squared local vol is a **stochastic convex
   combination** of the basic squared vols. Caplet (and, by linearity, *all Greeks*) =
   mixture/convex combination of basic caplet prices `Σλ_i Cpl_i` (eq 10.24). Virtually unlimited
   parameters (watch over-parameterization).
4. **10.4 LM (lognormal-mixture, Brigo–Mercurio 2000a,b)** — basic densities lognormal
   (`v_i=σ_i(t)y`); Prop 10.4.1 gives explicit `ν(t,y)` (eq 10.27) and a **unique strong solution**;
   `ν²` a weighted average of `σ_i²` bounded below/above by positive constants (eq 10.29). Caplet
   `τ N̄ P Σλ_i Bl(K,F_j(0),V_i(T_{j−1}))` (eq 10.31) → **smile-shaped vol with a minimum exactly at
   the ATM strike `F_j(0)`, zero slope at ATM**; skew shapes only with zero ATM slope. ATM implied vol
   and a `σ̂(m)` expansion (moneyness `m=ln(F_j(0)/K)`) are explicit (eqs 10.32–10.34).
5. **10.5 Dynamics under other measures (Prop 10.5.1)** — drift terms of standard LFM form but with
   local-vol coefficients; needed to propagate the whole surface under one common measure for exotics.
6. **10.5.1 Decorrelation result** — as *any* LVM the instantaneous correlation is
   `Corr(dF_j, dν_j²)=1` (eq 10.35), yet the **terminal correlation with time-averaged squared vol is
   exactly 0**: `Corr(ν_j²(T,F(T)),F(T))=0` and `Corr(v_j²(T),F(T))=0` for all `T` (eq 10.36). Explained
   later (Ch12, §12.1.1): the LVM is the local-volatility projection of an uncertain-volatility model
   whose instantaneous vol-asset correlation is zero (Brigo 2002b; BMR 2004).
7. **10.6 Shifting the LM** — `F_j=α+F̄_j` (most general affine martingale-preserving transform);
   shifted-mixture-of-lognormals density; caplet `Σλ_i Bl(K−α,F_j(0)−α,V_i)` (eq 10.39); α shifts curve
   level, moves the minimum-strike, and adds ATM asymmetry without shifting the curve.
8. **10.7 LMDM (different means, Brigo–Mercurio–Sartorelli 2003)** — basic `G_i` lognormal with
   drifts `µ_i` (constrained `Σλ_i e^{M_i}=1`, eq 10.42); local vol has an extra term that may go
   negative, positivity conditions in Lemma 10.7.1 and unique-strong-solution in Prop 10.7.1; caplet
   `Σλ_i e^{M_i}Bl(K e^{−M_i}, F_j(0), V_i)` (eq 10.46). **Steeper, more skewed curves; minimum can
   move far off ATM** (enables non-zero ATM slope).
9. **10.8 HSDM (hyperbolic-sine mixture, Mercurio / BMS 2003)** — basic `G_i` are hyperbolic-sine
   processes `G_i(t)=β_i(t)sinh(∫α_i dW − L_i)` (Carr–Tari–Zariphopoulou); explicit density (10.48) &
   caplet price (10.49); Prop 10.8.1 unique strong solution; mixture caplet (10.52) gives **steep
   skews**.
10. **10.9 Market test (Euro caplet surface, 16 Dec 2002)** — 19 maturities; LM tested with 3
    densities (5 free params), LMDM & HSDM with 2 densities (4 and 5 params). Conclusions: **LM
    adequate for pure smiles; LMDM needed for asymmetric/non-zero ATM slope; HSDM best for strong
    skews**; steep low-strike short-maturity skews remain hardest.
11. **10.10 Second general class (Brigo–Mercurio 2003)** — `F_j(t)=h(t,W_t)` with h strictly
    increasing, positive, zero at −∞, and `F_j` a martingale (assumptions A1–A4). Yields a driftless
    local-vol SDE (10.54), closed-form marginal (10.56) and **explicit transition density (10.58)** —
    enabling future-caplet/hedging checks — and caplet as a 1-D integral (10.61).
12. **10.11 GBM mixture** — `h=Σψ_i e^{−β_i²t/2+β_i w}` = mixture of N perfectly-correlated driftless
    GBMs (eqs 10.62–10.63); local vol `σ̄` a stochastic weighted average of the `β_i` (not squares —
    an analogy noted vs LM); caplet fully explicit (10.67/10.68) but implied vols are **weird
    (increasing, concave)** → poor for calibration.
13. **10.12 GBM-mixture extension for skews** — allow *negative* combinators `ψ_i` with
    `sign(ψ_i β_i)=1` (eq 10.69) to recover skews, patching positivity below `w̄` with
    `f(w)=a·exp(bw+cw²)` matched in value/slope/curvature (ε, α, β machinery, eqs 10.70–10.72), then
    rebuilding a martingale `h̄_t(w)=E[h̄_{T_{j−1}}(W_{T_{j−1}})|W_t=w]` (explicit, eq 10.73) to restore
    no-arbitrage. Caplets closed for `K≥ε`.
14. **10.13 Dupire-à-la dynamics (general, BM 2003)** — given caplet prices `C_j(K)` satisfying
    no-arbitrage conditions B1–B4, Breeden–Litzenberger gives `h^{-1}_{T_{j−1}}(x)` (eq 10.74) and the
    forward rate `F_j(t)=h_t(W_t)` (10.77–10.78); **exactly recovers the market caplet smile**;
    dynamics in Cor 10.13.3; future caplet prices via numerical 1-D integral (10.80). Advantages over
    the LMDM explicit SDE: milder parameter restrictions, constant coefficients (parsimony), fast
    future-caplet checks. (In Ch9 this same general dynamics is the rate-market analogue of
    **Dupire 1994/1997**, which was for equity/FX — BM/Balland–Hughston extended it to rates where only
    one caplet maturity per forward rate exists.)

## Ch 11 — Stochastic-Volatility Models (for forward rates)

### Verified content and structure
General SVM framework (opening): `dF_j=a_j(t)ϕ(F_j)[V]^γ dZ_j`, `dV=a_V dt+b_V dW` (eq 11.1),
`γ∈{1/2,1}`, `Z_j`,`W` possibly correlated. **Zero rate-vol correlation ⇒ smile-shaped vols (min at
ATM — Renault–Touzi 1996 when ϕ=identity); skew needs one of three devices:** (i) non-zero
correlation, (ii) displaced diffusion `ϕ(x)=x+β`, (iii) non-linear `ϕ` e.g. `x^α` (`α∈(0,1)`).
Analytical tools: uncorrelated case → Hull–White conditioning (price for a V-path, average over
vol-path functional density); correlated case → characteristic function of `F_j` under `Q_j` +
Fourier inversion (Heston route). Sections:
- **11.1 Andersen–Brotherton-Ratcliffe (2001):** dynamics under the *spot-LIBOR measure* `Q_d`
  (eq 11.2): rate vol `σ_j√V ϕ(F_j)`, `dV=κ(θ−V)dt+εψ(V)dW`, **zero corr** `dZ_j dW=0` (keeps V drift
  measure-invariant; eq 11.3 under forward measure). Mean-reverting, positive (e.g. `ψ(x)=x^{3/4}`
  non-explosive, origin inaccessible). **Caplets:** start from `G_j` satisfying a Feynman–Kac PDE
  (11.5); asymptotic expansion of implied vol in powers of `δ=T_{j−1}−t` (eqs 11.6–11.7, `Ω_0`, `Ω_1`
  from Bernoulli-type ODEs); stochastic vol re-introduced via HW decomposition + 4th-order Taylor in
  the integrated variance `U_j` (eq 11.8) using the Laplace transform of `U_j`'s density; final double
  expansion in `ε, δ` (eq 11.9–11.10) → explicit implied-vol estimate. **Swaptions:** weight-freezing
  on swap rates gives `w_j(t)` and a one-dimensional deterministic effective `σ_{α,β}`, so the caplet
  machinery prices swaptions by substitution.
- **11.2 Wu–Zhang (2002) (Heston-type):** rate vol `√V`, `dV=κ(θ−V)dt+ε√V dW` (square-root), with
  **non-zero correlation** `ρ_j(t)` (eq 11.12) under the risk-neutral `Q`. σ_j control smile level,
  ε curvature, ρ_j ATM slope. Because of correlation, moving to `Q^j` adds a drift `ξ_j(t)V` to V
  (eq 11.13, `ξ_j` sum over forward rates); tractability regained by **freezing forward rates at 0**
  to make V a pure square-root process. **Caplets via characteristic/MGF `φ`** solving a Feynman–Kac
  PDE (11.16) with `φ=exp(A+BV+zx)`; A,B explicit piecewise-constant coefficients (Heston Riccati
  form); prices via Fourier inversion integrals (eq 11.15). Swaptions: freeze again, swap rate
  `S_{α,β}` follows the same form (11.17) with substitutions (11.18).
- **11.3 Piterbarg (2003):** shifted-lognormal forward rates with stochastic squared vol: square-root
  `dV=κ(V(0)−V)dt+ε√V dW`, **mean-reverting to its own initial value, uncorrelated with rates**
  (eq 11.19); **ATM slopes controlled by the shift functions `b_j` (not by correlation)**; σ_j →
  smile level, ε → curvature. Distinguishing contribution: accurate European option prices under
  **general time-dependent** coefficients (not only piecewise constant). Swap-rate SDE approximated
  (11.20→11.21), least-squares effective `b_{α,β}(t)` (eq 11.24) and **effective constant (λ̄,b̄)**
  via a "skew average" weighting `ω(t)` (11.26) and matching ATM prices with the Laplace transform of
  the integrated variance (11.27–11.29). **Calibration = fit `b̄_{α,β}`→`b*`, `λ̄_{α,β}`→`λ*`**
  directly (no option-price approximation); 3-step scheme (vols→skews→recalibrate).
- **11.4 HKLW (2002) = SABR:** `dF_j = V F_j^β dZ`, `dV=εV dW`, `V(0)=α`, corr ρ (eq 11.31),
  β∈(0,1]. Acronym (stochastic, **A**lpha, **B**eta, **R**ho). `F_j` always a martingale for β<1; for
  β=1 martingale iff ρ≤0 (Jourdain 2004). **Singular-perturbation implied-vol approximation**
  (11.33) and explicit **ATM vol** (11.34), leading term `α/F^{1−β}` defining the **backbone**; the
  ln-strike expansion (11.35) separates the slope into **beta skew ∝ −½(1−β)** and **vanna skew ∝
  ½ρλ** (ρ<0 → downward slope), and curvature into beta-skew² + **volga ∝ λ²**, λ=εF^{(1−β)}/α.
  β chosen by history/regression on `(ln F_0, ln σ_ATM)` or a-priori, since market smiles fit many β.
  **Notable (Remark 11.4.1): SABR models a single forward asset and is NOT an LFM extension** — the
  joint/measure structure of a true LIBOR model is unspecified. Swaption use: replace `F_j→S_{a,b}`
  (eq 11.36) and treat (11.36) as exact (market practice). Calibration example to EUR data
  28-Sep-2005: smile quoted as `∆σ^{M}(±25…±200bp)`; per-pair (α,ε,ρ), common β=0.5; errors in
  Table 11.4.
- **11.5 Joshi–Rebonato (2003):** empirically-motivated, **not analytically tractable** (even
  caplets by MC). ATM implied vols appear ≈ CEV: `Y(t)=σ^{imp}(t)[S(t)]^β` has low variability (1×1
  swaption). Model = CEV per rate replaced (CEV↔displaced-diffusion equivalence, Marris/Muck) by a
  **shifted-lognormal** `dF_j=σ_j(t)[F_j+α_j]dZ` (eq 11.37 + shift) whose vol is a humped functional
  form `σ_j(t)=[a(t)+b(t)(T_{j−1}−t)]e^{−c(t)(T_{j−1}−t)}+d(t)` with **stochastic coefficients**
  `a,b,c,d` following OU processes (11.38), independent of rate noise (so identical under any
  forward measure). Caplets: HW-type integral of adjusted Black prices over the (MC-simulated)
  root-mean-square-vol density (11.39); swaption via freeze + one integral. Practical notes: the same
  volatility path serves many horizons; many strikes priced from one vol distribution.

## Ch 12 — Uncertain-Parameter Models

### Verified content and structure
- **UVM core (opening, §12 intro):** uncertain-volatility model = volatility is a *random variable*,
  not a diffusion: `dF_t=σ F_t dZ_t` (12.1), σ discrete independent of Z taking `σ_1..σ_N` w.p.
  `λ_1..λ_N`, **drawn at an infinitesimal time after zero**. Equivalent to a Black model with several
  vol scenarios. Independence ⇒ F-density is a mixture of lognormals, caplets = mixtures of Black
  caplet prices, implied vol **smile-shaped with a minimum at `F_0`**. Skews via shift:
  `dF_t=σ(F_t+α)dZ_t` (12.2) with (α,σ) a discrete random vector. Formulation (12.2) suggested
  (1-factor) by **Gatarek 2003**, and independently for a general asset by **Brigo–Mercurio–Rapisarda
  2004**; the multi-factor LFM extension reviewed is **Errais–Mauri–Mercurio 2004** (Gatarek's
  1-factor generalised). **Distributional analogy (footnote/Remark):** (12.1)'s UVM and the LVM
  (10.28) share the *same marginal density* but differ in transition densities, exotic-option prices
  and forward/future implied vols. Advantages: tractable, easy, flexible (caps+swaptions). Drawback:
  **future implied vols lose the initial smile almost immediately** after the draw (vol becomes flat
  if shifts are zero); empirically the model's forward vols stay close to current ones.
- **12.1 SLMUP (multi-factor):** under spot-LIBOR `Q^d`, rate vol `σ_j^I(t)(F_j(t)+α_j^I)` with I a
  random index (eq 12.3→12.4), forward-measure form (12.5); I independent of all noise so its law is
  measure-invariant. **12.1.1 Link to the lognormal-mixture LVM:** with `α_j^I=0`,
  `ν_j²(t,x)=E[σ_j^I(t)²|F_j(t)=x]` (Prop 12.1.1) — the Ch10 LVM (Prop 10.5.1) is the local-volatility
  version of the uncertain-volatility dynamics, which **explains the "zero terminal correlation"**
  result of §10.5.1 (Remark 12.1.1).
- **12.2 Calibration to caplets:** by iterated conditioning (12.6), caplet =
  `Σ_i λ_i Bl(K+α_j^i, F_j(0)+α_j^i, V_j^i)` (12.7), a **mixture of adjusted Black prices**;
  calibrate `Φ_j={V_j^i, α_j^i}` by least-squares over strikes (g=(x−y)²), sequentially j=1..M for
  fixed λ_i; recover point vols `σ_j^i=V_j^i/√T_{j−1}` (or fit a humped functional form 6.11-type).
  (Mercurio–Morini 2005: a special SLMUP auto-calibrates ATM.)
- **12.3 Swaption pricing:** swap rate `S_{α,β}` = martingale under `Q^{α,β}`; freezing the weights
  ⇒ approx dynamics shifted-log-normal-per-scenario, `dS=(S+η^I_{α,β})γ^I dW`; payer swaption =
  `Σ_h τ_h P(0,T_h) Σ_i λ_i Bl(K+η^i_{α,β}, S_{α,β}(0)+η^i_{α,β}, Γ^i_{α,β})` (**12.8**), a mixture of
  adjusted Black swaption prices; `Γ^i` is the integrated squared effective vol.
- **12.4 MC swaption pricing:** simulate under `Q^d`; logarithms `F̄_k^{α,I}=F_k+α_k^I` have
  deterministic diffusion conditional on I (Euler ≡ Milstein); price as sample mean of normalized
  payoff; report 99% CI (2.576 σ/√n); compare model implied vol `σ^{SLMUP}` and `σ^{MC}`.
- **12.5 Calibration to swaptions (two-stage):** after fixing σ,α from caps, use *instantaneous
  correlations* as free params to fit ATM swaptions. Semi-annual→annual tenor conversion (weights
  `u_1,u_2`; set annual α = semi-annual α). Correlation parametrizations tested: a two-parameter
  form `ρ_{i,j}=ρ̄+(1−ρ̄)sin(π/2)exp(−a|T_j−T_i|/T̄)` (**12.9**) and **Rebonato's**
  `ρ_{i,j}=ρ̄+(1−ρ̄)e^{−a|T_j−T_i|}` (**12.10**); PSD analysis by Taylor expansion (both converge to
  a PSD matrix); fit `Ψ=(ρ̄,a)` to ATM swaptions.
- **12.6 Calibration to market data:** Euro caps, **11-Aug-2004**, six-month-tenor caplets
  (strikes 2.5–7%, maturities 2–15y). Scenarios N=2,3,4 → **three scenarios best** (two underfits the
  skew; four adds nothing); priors λ=(0.6,0.3,0.1). Swaption calibration on Euro ATM vols 11-Aug-2004
  with the two correlation forms (Tables 12.4–12.8).
- **12.7 Testing the swaption approximation:** 2×5, 3×5, 4×5, 5×5 payer swaptions across strikes;
  the analytical approximation (12.8) **lies inside the 99% MC confidence interval for every strike**
  (prices and implied vols).
- **12.8 Further model implications:** (i) the model **induces a swaption smile/skew from the cap
  skew + ATM swaption vols** — a guide for quoting OTM/ITM swaptions absent quotes (Fig 12.8); (ii)
  it produces **self-similar forward caplet volatilities** — good for hedging exotics; forward-start
  caplet (FSC) pricing per Rubinstein 1991 (payoff `τ_k[F_k(T_{k−1})−δF_k(T_j)]^+`, eq 12.12), giving
  forward implied vols that stay near current ones (Fig 12.9).
- **12.9 Joint calibration to caps+swaptions:** EUR **30-Dec-2004**; N=3; vol parametrized by
  humped form (6.11); common shift per scenario; a **4-parameter correlation generalization of
  Rebonato** `ρ_{i,j}=a+(1−a)exp(−|i−j|(b−c·max(i,j)+d·max(i,j)²))` (**12.13**). Total **243 free
  parameters** = 234 vol (39×3×2) + 3 shifts + 4 correlation + 2 λ's. Calibrate by minimising summed
  squared percentage price differences; seed with fast sequential cap fit, then global optimization
  over caps+swaptions. Errors small (esp. caps); largest residuals for long-maturity/short-tenor
  swaptions (known difficulty of fitting one-year-tenor caplets vs swaptions together).
