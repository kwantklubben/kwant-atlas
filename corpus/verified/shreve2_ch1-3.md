# CHAPTER 1 — GENERAL PROBABILITY THEORY (printed pp.1–47; PNG 21–67)

## Key concepts (verified)
- Infinite probability spaces motivated by two canonical experiments: (a) choosing a point uniformly at random in `(0,1]` and (b) tossing a coin infinitely often. **Ω_∞ (infinite sequences of H/T) is uncountable** — this is why no single outcome can be assigned positive mass and why the theory must work on sets (σ-algebras) rather than outcomes.
- **Def 1.1.1 (σ-algebra):** `F` on nonempty Ω contains `∅`, is closed under complements and countable unions (⇒ countable intersections, finite unions/intersections, `Ω∈F`). Borel σ-algebra `B(IR)`; on a finite Ω one takes all subsets.
- **Def 1.1.2 (probability measure):** `P:F→[0,1]`, `P(Ω)=1`, **countable additivity** `P(∪_n A_n)=Σ_n P(A_n)` for disjoint `A_n` (⇒ finite additivity, `P(A^c)=1−P(A)`, `P(∅)=0`).
- **Example 1.1.3 — Uniform (Lebesgue) measure on [0,1]:** defined by `P(a,b]=b−a`, extended to Borel sets by countable additivity.
- Random variables and **distribution measures**: Def 1.2.3 `μ_X(B)=P{X∈B}`; a random variable and its distribution are distinct objects (two RV's can share a law; one RV can have two laws under two measures).
- Expectations on uncountable spaces = Lebesgue integral (partition the *y*-axis); the "standard machine" (indicator → simple → nonneg → general); MCT, Fatou, DCT (Thms 1.4.5/1.4.9 and analogues); computation of expectations via densities (Thm 1.5.1).
- **§1.6 Change of measure** and the Radon–Nikodym construction.

## Key results (verified)
- **Radon–Nikodym derivative / change of measure (Thm 1.6.1):** let `Z≥0` a.s., `E Z=1`; set `P̃(A)=∫_A Z dP`. Then `P̃` is a probability measure, `P̃≪P`; if additionally `Z>0` a.s. then `P̃` and `P` are **equivalent**. Change-of-expectation: `Ẽ X = E[XZ]` (nonneg/integrable X), and for `Z>0`: `E Y = Ẽ[Y/Z]`. Book records them as `∫X dP̃ = ∫ X Z dP` and inverse.
- **Example 1.6.x — recentering a normal under a change of measure (preview of Girsanov).** X standard normal under P. With `Z(ω)=exp{−θX(ω)−½θ²}` (θ a positive constant), `E Z=1`, define `P̃(A)=∫_A Z dP`. Under `P̃`, **`Y=X+θ` is standard normal.** (Completing the square: integrand `exp{−θx−θ²/2}φ(x)=exp{−½(x+θ)²}/√(2π)`.) *Verified verbatim on PNG p-057/p-058; parameter there is θ.*
- Measure-theoretic convergence/limit theorems (MCT/Fatou/DCT) justify passing limits through expectations — used repeatedly in Ch.3.
- Risk-neutral measures exist and are equivalent to the market measure (bridge to continuous-time HJM condition, noted at end of §1.7).

# CHAPTER 2 — INFORMATION AND CONDITIONING (printed pp.49–81; PNG 69–101)

## Key concepts (verified)
- **§2.1 Information = σ-algebra.** Def of σ-algebra generated by a collection / by random variables; atoms on a finite space `F_n` built up from coin-toss prefixes (Example: `F_1={∅,Ω,A_H,A_T}`, `F_2` with 16 sets, etc.); σ(X) = information revealed by X.
- **§2.2 Independence** of sets, σ-algebras, and random variables; criterion in terms of joint cdf / joint MGF factoring (Thm 2.2.7(vi)); *uncorrelated ≠ independent* example (X standard normal, Z=±1 equally, Y=ZX: Y is standard normal, X,Y uncorrelated but not independent and **have no joint density**); jointly normal random variables (Def 2.2.11) and their joint density.
- **§2.3 General (measure-theoretic) conditional expectation** via **partial averaging** (Definition 2.3.1); existence & uniqueness a.s. (stated as Thm 2.3.1 via Radon–Nikodym on the previous page, PNG p-089).
- **§2.4 Properties** of conditional expectation (below); conditional densities; the Independence Lemma.

## Key results (verified)
- **Definition 2.3.1 (partial averaging):** `E[X|G]` is `G`-measurable and `∫_A E[X|G] dP = ∫_A X dP` for every `A∈G`. Existence/uniqueness up to a.s.-equality; reduces to the binomial "average over atoms" picture when `G` is generated by atoms.
- **Properties (numbered (i)–(v) in Thm 2.3.2; eqs 2.3.18–2.3.22):**
  - (i) **Linearity** `E[c₁X+c₂Y|G]=c₁E[X|G]+c₂E[Y|G]`.
  - (ii) **Taking out what is known:** X `G`-measurable ⇒ `E[XY|G]=X·E[Y|G]`.
  - (iii) **Iterated conditioning (tower):** H⊆G ⇒ `E[E[X|G]|H]=E[X|H]`.
  - (iv) **Independence:** X independent of G ⇒ `E[X|G]=EX`.
  - (v) **Conditional Jensen:** φ convex ⇒ `E[φ(X)|G] ≥ φ(E[X|G])`.
- **Independence Lemma (Lemma 2.3.4, general form):** X₁,…,X_K `G`-measurable, Y₁,…,Y_L independent of `G`, `f` bounded Borel ⇒ `E[f(X₁,…,X_K,Y₁,…,Y_L)|G] = g(X₁,…,X_K)` where `g(x₁,…,x_K)=E[f(x₁,…,x_K,Y₁,…,Y_L)]`. *Workhorse for the Markov property (used in §3.5).*
- Conditional densities (ratio of joint to marginal, normal example `E[Y|X]` as best linear/unbiased estimator).

# CHAPTER 3 — BROWNIAN MOTION (printed pp.83–123; PNG 103–143)

## Key concepts (verified)
- Symmetric random walk M_k → **scaled symmetric random walk** `W^{(n)}(t)` (step ±1/√n every 1/n) → Brownian motion via a Donsker-type functional CLT (Thm 3.2.1); increments i.i.d. mean 0, variance = elapsed time; RW is a martingale; its quadratic variation up to k is k; scaling gives `W^{(n)}(t)→N(0,t)`.
- **§3.2.7 Log-normal limit of the binomial model (r=0):** S_n(t) of the binomial converges to geometric-Brownian log-normal `S(t)=S(0)exp{σW(t)−½σ²t}` (Thm 3.2.2), `W(t)~N(0,t)`.
- **Def 3.3.1 (Brownian motion):** continuous `W(t), t≥0`, `W(0)=0`, increments `W(t_{i+1})−W(t_i)` independent and normal with mean 0, variance `t_{i+1}−t_i`.
- Alternative characterizations (Thm 3.3.2): (independent normal increments) ⇔ (jointly normal with covariance `E[W(s)W(t)]=min(s,t)=s` for `s<t`) ⇔ (joint MGF form (3.3.5)).
- **Filtration for BM (Def 3.3.3):** increasing `F(t)`; `W(t)` adapted; and (independence of the future) `W(u)−W(t)` independent of `F(t)` for `u≥t` (⇒ efficient-markets intuition).
- **Martingale property (Thm 3.3.4):** `E[W(t)|F(s)]=W(s)`.
- **§3.4 Quadratic variation:** first-order variation `FV_T(f)=∫_0^T |f'|dt` (BM has *infinite* first-order variation); QV of BM accumulates **at rate 1 per unit time**; informal rules `dW·dW=dt`, `dW·dt=0`, `dt·dt=0`.
- **§3.4.3 Volatility of geometric BM:** volatility σ recovered from squared log-returns of the price.
- **§3.5 Markov property** with transition density `p(τ,x,y)`.
- **§3.6/§3.7 First passage times and reflection principle** (with drift case deferred to Exercise 3.7 and Ch.7 via Girsanov).

## Key results (verified — formula-critical pages vision-read)
- **Exponential martingale (Thm 3.6.1, eq 3.6.1):** with σ a constant, `Z(t)=exp{σW(t)−½σ²t}` is a P-martingale w.r.t. the BM filtration. *Note: the book's parameter letter is σ here (not θ as in the existing extraction).*
- **First passage time** `τ_m = min{t≥0: W(t)=m}` (∞ if never reached), eq (3.6.3). For m>0, stopping the exponential martingale (`t∧τ_m`) and dominated/monotone convergence give:
  - `E[ 1_{τ_m<∞} exp{−½σ²τ_m} ] = e^{−σ m}` (eq 3.6.6), hence `P{τ_m<∞}=1` (eq 3.6.7), and `E[exp{−½σ²τ_m}]=e^{−σ m}` (eq 3.6.8).
  - **Laplace transform (Thm 3.6.2, eq 3.6.9):** `E e^{−ατ_m} = e^{−|m|√(2α)}` for all `α>0` (set `σ=√(2α)`).
  - **Remark 3.6.3:** differentiating gives `E[τ_m e^{−ατ_m}]= (|m|/√(2α)) e^{−|m|√(2α)}`; letting `α↓0`: **`E τ_m = ∞` for `m≠0`** (though τ_m is a.s. finite).
- **Reflection equality (eq 3.7.1, §3.7.1):** for w≤m, m>0: `P{τ_m≤t, W(t)≤w} = P{W(t) ≥ 2m−w}`. In terms of the running maximum `M(t)=max_{0≤s≤t}W(s)` (eq 3.7.5), `M(t)≥m ⇔ τ_m≤t`, so `P{M(t)≥m, W(t)≤w} = P{W(t)≥2m−w}` (eq 3.7.6).
- **First-passage cdf and density (Thm 3.7.1, eqs 3.7.2–3.7.3):** `P{τ_m≤t} = 2P{W(t)≥|m|} = 2[1−Φ(|m|/√t)]` and density
  `f_{τ_m}(t) = |m|/(t√(2πt)) · e^{−m²/(2t)} = |m|/√(2π t³) · e^{−m²/(2t)}, t>0`. *(Equivalent forms; verified.)*
- **Joint density of (M(t),W(t)) (Thm 3.7.3, eq 3.7.7):** for w≤m, m>0:
  `f_{M(t),W(t)}(m,w) = (2(2m−w))/(t√(2πt)) · e^{−(2m−w)²/(2t)}`.
- **Conditional density of the maximum given the terminal value (Cor 3.7.4):** given `W(t)=w` (so `M(t)≥w`), for w≤m, m>0:
  `f_{M(t)|W(t)}(m|w) = (2(2m−w)/t) · e^{−2m(m−w)/t}`.
- **Markov property / transition density (§3.5):** `p(τ,x,y) = (1/√(2πτ)) exp{−(y−x)²/(2τ)}`, with `E[f(W(t))|F(s)] = ∫f(y) p(t−s, W(s), y) dy` (eq 3.5.5) — the only relevant information from `F(s)` is `W(s)`.
- **Quadratic variation (Def 3.4.1, Thm 3.4.3):** `[f,f](T)=lim_{‖Π‖→0} Σ_j (f(t_{j+1})−f(t_j))²`; for BM the limit is `T` (mean of each squared increment is `t_{j+1}−t_j`, variance `2(t_{j+1}−t_j)²`, LLN ⇒ Σ→T). Informal QV bookkeeping: eq (3.4.10) `dW dW = dt`; (3.4.12) `dW dt=0`; (3.4.13) `dt dt=0`.
- **Geometric BM (§3.2.7, §3.4.3):** `S(t)=S(0)exp{σW(t)+(α−½σ²)t}` with `W` the driving BM (log-normal); σ is the volatility and is identified from squared log-price differences, `Σ_j (log S(t_{j+1})−log S(t_j))² ≈ σ²(T₂−T₁)`.
